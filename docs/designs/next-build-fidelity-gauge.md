# Design: Build the fidelity gauge before tuning the dial

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-07
Branch: test
Repo: AfterlifeSystems/Neural-Nexus-Frontend
Status: DRAFT
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement

Neural Nexus has thirteen candidate features on `todo.md` and no principle for ordering
them. The founder asked one question: of everything on that list, what should be built
next?

The list was written entirely from the founder's own judgment. No item traces to a
person who asked for it. Meanwhile 228 changed files sit unlanded on branch `test`
representing roughly nine months of work — voice mode, geo-located avatars, emotion
media, the research panel, connectors, the agent inbox, response metrics — none of
which any person outside the team has used.

The real problem is not which feature. It is that the product currently produces no
evidence, so any ordering of the list defends itself equally well and none can be
proven wrong.

## Demand Evidence

None. Stated plainly because it is the central fact of this design.

- Zero users outside the team.
- Zero feature requests. The founder confirmed the entire roadmap is his own judgment.
- No revenue, no waitlist behavior, no one who would be upset if it disappeared.

This is a pre-product build with nine months of features already in it. That inversion —
lots of product, no evidence — is what the recommendation below is designed to correct.

## Status Quo

The founder identified the status quo as **people revisiting old media**: photos,
videos, voicemails, saved messages. The raw material of who a person is already exists
in their own media, and today nothing uses it.

Notably not the status quo: a free custom GPT with a long personality prompt. That was
this session's proposed answer and the founder rejected it. Worth revisiting only if
the "sounds like them" claim ever needs defending against a free substitute.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The founder named five personas rather than one human:

1. Convenience of a personal secretary managing their digital life
2. Connecting to the likeness of their friends
3. Wanting to be remembered
4. Wanting to know more about themselves
5. Preferring an LLM over a person for shopping

These do not divide into "consumer vs business." They divide by whether fidelity is
the product:

| Persona | Is fidelity the product? | What they actually buy |
|---|---|---|
| Personal secretary | No | Reliability |
| Friends' likeness | **Yes** | Presence |
| Being remembered | **Yes** | Permanence |
| Knowing yourself | **Yes** | Insight from own data |
| Shopping via LLM | No | Catalog, prices, checkout |

Personas 1 and 5 do not need the identity pipeline. A secretary that sounds exactly
like you but books the wrong day is worse than useless; shopping needs a catalog and
payments. Both could be built without a line of the fidelity work.

Personas 2, 3, and 4 are the only ones where "it sounds exactly like them" *is* the
product — and every one of the 228 unlanded files is an investment in fidelity. **The
codebase has already chosen. The roadmap has not**, which is why it holds thirteen
items instead of one.

The wedge was not narrowed to a single named human during this session. See Open
Questions.

## Constraints

- Pre-product, zero users. Evidence is the scarce resource, not code.
- 228 files unlanded on `test`, actively edited by two concurrent sessions. Merge risk
  compounds weekly.
- Fine-tuning Llama-Maverick-17B via Together is the most expensive item on the list.
- A ratings/feedback backend is already in flight from a parallel session:
  `POST /message_feedback` and `GET /avatar_preferences/{assistant_id}`.
- Anonymous-user support already exists (`get_current_user_or_anonymous_user`).
- Sharing rails already exist and are unused: `create_avatar`, `share_avatar`,
  `list_public_avatars`, `select_avatar`.

## Premises

1. Adapters improve fidelity, and nothing in the product currently measures fidelity.
   Tuning a dial with no gauge attached is unfalsifiable spending.
2. The five personas are not one product. Sequencing that serves all five produces a
   wider product and the same evidence as today, which is none.
3. The fidelity work already paid for (voice, emotion, diarization, research) only
   returns value under personas 2, 3, and 4.
4. Amount of data and quality of data are the two cheapest authenticity levers.
   Adapters are the fourth and most expensive.
5. A 228-file branch that never lands is not neutral. It accrues merge risk and hides
   defects that only real use would expose.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Land and instrument (RECOMMENDED, and already actioned)
Merge `test` to main. Finish the ratings and feedback loop already in flight. Put it
in front of five people and watch without helping.
Effort: S (human ~1 week / CC ~1-2 days). Risk: Low. Reuses: the entire branch.

### Approach B: The share loop
Invite-a-friend flow: a friend talks to your avatar without an account, then is
prompted to build their own. The only mechanic on the list where user #2 arrives
without recruiting.
Effort: M (human ~3-4 weeks / CC ~4-6 days). Risk: Med. Reuses: sharing tools,
anonymous user path, carousel, voice mode.

### Approach C: The mirror
Upload media, receive a written report about yourself. No chat, no account. Shareable
output as a second distribution path.
Effort: M (human ~3 weeks / CC ~3-5 days). Risk: Med. Reuses: ingestion pipeline,
`GeneralCharacteristicExtraction`, OCEAN analysis.
Not chosen: one-shot novelty with unsolved retention.

### Ruled out during the session
- **Adapters first** (as written at the top of `todo.md`): most expensive item on the
  list, moving a number nobody can currently see.
- **The entire `usefulness` bucket** (inbox, booking, bank/business URL analysis,
  twitch/discord/slack, LLM-as-a-service, phone calls, texting, ambient triage,
  avatar-initiated messages): serves personas 1 and 5, uses almost none of the fidelity
  investment. A later company, not a next build.
- **Lip-synced Live Mode video**: highest wow, lowest evidence yield. A launch asset,
  not a next build.

## Recommended Approach

**Build the gauge before tuning the dial.** Reorder the `quality` bucket to the
reverse of how it is written:

1. **Ratings and feedback** — next. Cheap, already half-built in a parallel session,
   and the only item that makes every other item measurable. The founder moved this to
   `# queued work` during the session.
2. **Social connection, pull, and scrape** — after ratings. Hits both cheap
   authenticity levers at once (more data, better data) and removes cold-start: a new
   user connects an account instead of hunting a hard drive for files.
3. **Fine-tuned adapters** — third, not first. Worth paying for once ratings can show
   whether the tuning worked and scrape has fed it better data.

Landing `test` is a precondition on all three, not a separate project.

## Open Questions

1. **No named human.** The wedge is still five personas wide. The design holds without
   it, but persona choice will bind as soon as ratings produce data — the rating that
   matters differs per persona.
2. **Which fidelity persona leads** (friends' likeness / being remembered / knowing
   yourself). Persona 2 is the only one with distribution in the mechanic. Unresolved.
3. **What "good" means on the ratings scale.** A thumbs-up on a helpful answer is not
   the same signal as "that sounds like her." If the gauge measures helpfulness, it
   will not measure fidelity, and the adapters decision stays unfalsifiable. Decide the
   dimension before the schema sets.
4. **Consent model for shared likenesses** if the share loop is built later.

## Success Criteria

- `test` merged to main; branch drift no longer accumulating.
- Ratings captured on real messages, readable per avatar, with a fidelity dimension
  distinct from a helpfulness dimension.
- Five people outside the team have used the product while the founder watched silently.
- At least one recorded surprise: something a user did that the product was not
  designed for.
- A baseline fidelity number that exists before any adapter is trained, so the adapter
  spend can be judged against it.

## Dependencies

- Parallel session's `POST /message_feedback` and `GET /avatar_preferences/{assistant_id}`
  routes, plus its uncommitted frontend edits to `MediaContext.jsx`, `MessageList.jsx`,
  `LiveVoiceMode.jsx`, `AmbientNotificationCard.jsx`, `avatarService.jsx`.
- Merging `test` blocks the clean measurement of anything.
- Store rating writes must stay consistent with the vector index config in
  `langgraph.json` if ratings are ever embedded.

## The Assignment

Not "go build ratings." That is already queued.

**Sit behind five people while they use Neural Nexus for the first time, and say
nothing.** Not a demo, not a walkthrough — hand it over and keep your mouth shut, even
when they get stuck. Especially then. Write down every place they hesitate and every
thing they try that you did not design for.

Do this in the same week ratings ships, so the qualitative and quantitative signals
arrive together. Five sessions of silence will tell you more than the next thirteen
features.
